01 · The idea

An agent on every site, an operations manager over them, and a specialist behind them.

Most AI products are building copilots: one smart assistant a person drives, like a navigation system in a delivery van. We can also give every site its own small agent, a minion, like a delivery drone attached to one box. It wakes up on a schedule, looks after that one site, remembers what it did last time, and answers the same way every time. The Asset Steward is that minion, an operations manager is how people talk to all of them, and a specialist agent learns from every site.

02 · How a minion finds out

The equipment notices first. The minion knows only what reaches Sitetracker.

A minion doesn’t watch the equipment itself. The equipment’s own sensors detect the fault and report it to the customer’s monitoring system. The minion sees it once that data reaches Sitetracker.

If a wire breaks, does anything notice?

What breaksNoticed?How
Power cable to the equipment cutYesIt goes silent. Its regular “I’m alive” message stops, so it’s marked offline.
Charging cable damagedUsuallySessions fail, or it reports a fault when someone plugs in.
Wire in a solar string brokenYes, as a dropThe inverter sees one string at zero and output falls.
Mains to a tower cutYesThe power system raises a “mains failure” alarm to the monitoring centre.
Cracked casing, loose cover, damage with no electrical effectNoNo sensor sees it. Found by people: a resident report, a tech visit, or Computer Vision in a job photo.
What this means

Faults like “9 trips” or “string offline” need the customer’s monitoring data, so that connection comes first (see SCADA). A nightly export is the quickest start for a pilot. Damage no sensor can see will always come through people and photos.

First question for each customer

“Which monitoring system do your chargers, inverters or sites report to, and can it export alarms to us daily?”

15 · Future scope

SCADA: the minion hears the equipment directly

Today the minion works from Sitetracker data, photos and monitoring exports, on a daily heartbeat. With SCADA and monitoring systems connected, live alarms and readings stream in, so it reacts to the equipment itself, the moment something changes.

TodayDaily heartbeat

Reads jobs, PM dates, photos and the last monitoring export. Finds a fault the next morning.

With SCADA (future)Event-driven

An alarm or reading wakes the minion straight away. The daily heartbeat stays as the safety net.

What it unlocks, per agent
  • Asset Health Live alarms and readings, not yesterday’s export. A fault wakes the minion the moment it happens.
  • PM Autopilot Run-hours and cycle counts from the equipment move PMs to actual use, not the calendar.
  • Field Verification After a repair, the live reading confirms the fix worked before the tech leaves.
  • Warranty & Billing The alarm history becomes evidence in the claim: when it failed, how often, what it read.
  • Parts Autopilot Early warnings (a battery drifting, a fan slowing) order the part before the failure.
For this customer

Inverter and meter data: output against expected, inverter fault codes, battery state.

Charger back-office alarms over OCPP, the EV standard: trips, offline posts, session failures. Brock is building this connector now.

Site power alarms: mains failure, battery voltage, rectifier faults, door open.

How we get there
  1. Now: the Scout Chrome extension reads monitoring and SCADA screens with no integration.
  2. Next: read-only feeds of alarms and readings into the site record.
  3. Later: full history, so health scores learn from real readings.
Guardrails
  • Read-only. The minion never sends commands to equipment: no remote resets, no switching.
  • Connected only with the customer’s IT and OT security approval.
  • If the feed drops, the slice shows “not reporting” and the daily heartbeat carries on.

Level 2

One tap

Drafts it fully; a person taps approve.

  • Repair moved onto a PM visit
  • Parts reserved on a van

Level 3

Drafts, you check

Writes the first version; a person edits and sends.

  • Warranty claims
  • Billable work on contracts
  • Commissioning sign-off pack
Level 4

Only people decide

It gives the facts and stops.

  • Repair or replace
  • Safe to energize
  • Anything sent to a customer or utility
Earned trust

Each action type starts one level stricter than it needs to be. When people approve an action type many times without editing it, the customer can move it up a level. If people start rejecting it, it moves back down. The customer always holds the dial; levels 3 and 4 never move without them.

  • Most PM Autopilot work starts on its own, every night: it fixes PM that silently failed to generate, groups the next 4 weeks of due work into visits, and checks that completed cycles rolled over. The results wait in one ranked list, the PM plan of the night, on the Maintenance home and in Scout.
  • When the maintenance planner can name the record and the action, it’s a button on the screen they already have open: a Maintenance Profile, a Field Asset or the Dispatch Console. Visits land on the Console as proposed bars to accept, move or decline.
  • Chat is for questions that need words, such as “what if we move South’s battery tests to Q1?”. In every case nothing is written without approval in v1, every proposal names its source, and every change can be undone.
  • Build order: first the plan of the night, in Sitetracker and Scout; then the buttons on Sitetracker screens; then everything else in Scout.

4 · Not just a query

Why this is an agent, not a report of what’s due

A list of what’s due tomorrow is a database query. PM Autopilot uses that list as its starting point, then plans.

A report tells youPM Autopilot does
What’s due tomorrowGroups due PMs and open repairs by place and date window, and proposes one visit instead of several.
Nothing about deadlinesRanks work by risk: contract or regulatory deadline, SLA penalty, asset health.
Nothing about the vanChecks parts and van space before it adds work, and picks a bigger vehicle when the load needs it.
Nothing about costShows what the plan saves: trips, miles and hours.
Missing jobs stay missingFinds PMs that never generated, or generated twice, and fixes the plan.
The same list every timeLearns: remembers site access windows, and suggests a longer interval when an asset never needs anything (a person decides).

How it creates jobs

  1. 1Reads

    PM plans and their generated jobs, open repairs, assets, sites, contracts, vans and stock.

  2. 2Checks

    Missing or duplicate PM jobs, and why. Fixes the plan link at level 1.

  3. 3Bundles

    Joins nearby due work into visits, within each job’s deadline.

  4. 4Loads

    Asks Parts Autopilot for parts and checks van space.

  5. 5Proposes

    One plan card per region: visits, parts, the saving. A person approves.

  6. 6Writes

    Updates the jobs and hands them to the Dispatch agent for people and times.

Guardrails
  • Never cancels or skips a PM required by contract or regulation.
  • Suggesting a longer interval needs the asset’s history, and a person decides (level 4).
  • Changes jobs, never deletes them. Every change is logged and can be undone.
  • The Dispatch agent still decides who goes and when.

Why it’s the legs

Legs carry the work forward in time. It takes what’s coming due and walks it through to done. It absorbs four earlier ideas (PM Bundling, the PM Visit Planner, the PM Generation Doctor and PM parts reservation) into one agent with one owner. That’s the difference between a portfolio and a product.

Buildable because it’s deterministic

Every branch of PM generation is already documented. Scout follows the rules. The language model only writes the dispatcher-facing explanation of the plan. Autonomy grows in steps: the skateboard asks the dispatcher to approve each plan.

11 · Business case

How it brings business

For our customers
  • Compliance and SLAs protected. Missed inspections are the pain customers named: sites missed, visits not batched, no defensible record that statutory inspections happened on time.
  • Uptime rules met. For EV operators, uptime is regulated. UK rules require 99% reliability for public rapid chargers.
  • Fewer truck rolls. Due work at one site is done in one trip. In the mock, 18 due tasks become 6 visits.
  • Planner time back. From building the plan to reviewing exceptions.
For Sitetracker

How photos are checked (Computer Vision)

  1. 1Checklist

    Before the visit, builds the photo list from the job template: e.g. wide shot, old part, new nameplate, ID label, finished work, screen.

  2. 2Capture

    The tech takes or uploads each photo in the mobile app. Each one is checked as it lands.

  3. 3Quality

    In focus, lit, not blocked. If not, a retake is asked for while the tech is still on site.

  4. 4Identify and read

    Recognises what is in the photo and reads text: serials, models, ratings, post IDs, screen status.

  5. 5Check

    Compares with the checklist and the record: right asset, right part, nothing missing. Below 90% confidence a person confirms.

  6. 6Act

    Updates the record, flags defects (they become evidence for Warranty & Billing), adds it to the quarterly report.

9 · Definition and skateboard

What it is, and the smallest version that works every time

Who uses it
Billing or warranty coordinator, and finance
Goal
Every job against an asset is classified and recovered: claim, contract-covered, billable or goodwill.
Starts when
A job against an asset closes, or a regulatory inspection comes due
Reads
The asset’s warranty window · stf_Contract_Agreement__c and stf_Obligation__c (Agreement Central) · job photos, forms and parts
Writes
Classification on the job · evidence pack · warranty claim or invoice line · certificate filed against the asset
Never does
Files a claim or bills a customer without approval
Why it’s the arm

It reaches outside Sitetracker, to the maker’s warranty portal and the customer’s invoice. In v1 a person approves and files. It’s a revenue agent: every other idea here cuts cost, and this one pays for the program.
